In New Mexico, | if you're walking home alone after midnight, | and a woman in black is standing at the crossroads... | keep walking. | Because she isn't waiting for a ride. | She's waiting to tell you who's next. | They call her La Mala Hora. | The evil hour. | Her story is told in Hispanic communities across New Mexico, | where white crosses called descansos line the back roads. | Each one marks a place where someone died. | According to the legend, she appears to people traveling alone at night, | right where two roads meet. | She never touches you. | She doesn't have to. | But here's the terrifying part. | Seeing her doesn't mean you'll die. | It means someone you love will. | One woman saw her at a crossroads. | By morning, she learned her husband had died... | at that exact hour. | They say she doesn't always look human. | Sometimes she's just a dark shape rolling across the road. | So if you're walking home alone tonight, | don't stop, and don't look back. | Because she isn't there for you. | She's there to tell you who's not coming home.
